1 · Invitation to Consult

Beginning the client relationship

To: BUS 101 Consulting Team
From: Emily Carter, Marcus Reed, and Sarah Mitchell
Co-Founders, EverWeave

EverWeave is a Michigan-based outdoor apparel company founded by three friends who shared a passion for the outdoors and a belief that quality products and thoughtful business practices could coexist. Today, we design and sell U.S.-made outdoor apparel and accessories for customers who hike, bike, paddle, camp, and simply enjoy being outside. Our products are sold directly to customers and through independent retailers across the country.

EverWeave has reached an important point in our company’s growth, and we are seeking an outside perspective as we consider a new business opportunity and what it could mean for our future.

Since founding EverWeave, we have worked to build a company grounded in quality products, strong relationships, and thoughtful decision-making. Our growth has created opportunities we once only imagined, but we also recognize that every opportunity deserves careful consideration. The decisions we make today can influence our employees, customers, business partners, and the direction of our company for years to come.

We are inviting your consulting team to work with us as we evaluate this opportunity. If you choose to accept this engagement, your team will receive additional information about EverWeave, the opportunity before us, and the questions we are considering. We will ask you to independently analyze that information and ultimately provide us with a well-supported recommendation.

If your team is interested in working with EverWeave, please respond in writing confirming your intent to accept the consulting engagement. We view your response as the beginning of our professional relationship and look forward to learning more about the team we may be working with.

We look forward to hearing from you.

Emily Carter, Marcus Reed & Sarah Mitchell
Co-Founders
EverWeave

2 · Professional Response to EverWeave

Purpose

Your consulting team has received an invitation from EverWeave to assist the company with an important business decision. Before receiving the full consulting engagement, your team must respond professionally to EverWeave’s invitation.

This is your team’s first communication with a potential client. Consider not only what you want to communicate, but how your writing represents your team.

Your Task

Prepare a professional written response to Emily Carter, Marcus Reed, and Sarah Mitchell confirming your team’s intent to accept the EverWeave consulting engagement.

Your response should:

  • Clearly communicate your team’s decision to accept the engagement.
  • Acknowledge your understanding of the general purpose of the consulting work.
  • Introduce your consulting team appropriately.
  • Demonstrate a professional tone appropriate for communication with a client.
  • Use clear, concise, and well-organized writing.
  • Be carefully proofread for grammar, spelling, punctuation, and formatting.

Your team will receive additional information about EverWeave and the consulting engagement after your response has been submitted.

Before You Submit

Read your response from the client’s perspective.

If this were the first communication you received from a consulting team, what would it tell you about the people you were about to work with?

3 · EverWeave Consulting Engagement

Acknowledgment after the team accepts the invitation

To: BUS 101 Consulting Team
From: Emily Carter, Marcus Reed, and Sarah Mitchell
Co-Founders, EverWeave

Thank you for agreeing to work with EverWeave. We appreciate your willingness to bring an outside perspective to an important decision for our company, and we look forward to working with your team.

As we shared in our initial invitation, EverWeave has reached a point in our growth where new opportunities require us to think carefully about what comes next. One such opportunity has recently been presented by Summit Trail Outfitters, a national outdoor retailer interested in establishing a long-term retail partnership with EverWeave.

The Summit proposal could significantly expand access to our products and introduce EverWeave to new customers across the country. It would also require meaningful commitments of resources, people, and organizational capacity. As founders, we recognize both the potential of the opportunity and our responsibility to understand its broader implications before making a decision.

We are asking your team to independently evaluate the Summit Trail Outfitters proposal and recommend how EverWeave should proceed. The materials provided to you include background information about EverWeave, details of the Summit proposal, and business and financial information relevant to your analysis.

Please treat the information provided for this engagement as confidential and use it only for purposes of completing your consulting work.

Apply critical thinking as you evaluate the available evidence, consider meaningful alternatives, and develop a well-supported recommendation.

As you conduct your work, consider the potential benefits of your recommendation as well as the resources required, tradeoffs involved, risks created, and implications for the people and relationships connected to EverWeave. We also expect you to consider how your recommendation could be implemented successfully.

We are not looking for a particular answer. We are looking for your best thinking.

Your work should help us understand not simply what EverWeave should do next, but why your proposed course of action is appropriate for the company we have built and the company we hope to become.

Thank you for accepting this engagement. We look forward to reviewing your recommendation.

Emily Carter, Marcus Reed & Sarah Mitchell
Co-Founders
EverWeave

Business Terms Reference

The following terms may be helpful as you analyze the EverWeave case. These are common business terms and are provided as a reference rather than as solutions to the case.

Revenue

The total money generated from selling products or services before expenses are deducted. Revenue is not the amount of money the owners of a business personally receive.

Gross Margin

The percentage of sales revenue remaining after subtracting the direct cost of the products sold. Gross margin does not account for all of a company’s operating expenses and is not the same as operating margin or final profit.

Gross Profit

The amount of revenue remaining after subtracting the direct costs associated with producing or purchasing the products sold.

Operating Expenses

The costs required to run a business, such as employee compensation, facilities, marketing, technology, insurance, and other normal business expenses.

Operating Income

The amount remaining after the direct costs of products and normal operating expenses are deducted from revenue.

Operating Margin

The percentage of revenue remaining as operating income after the normal costs of producing products and operating the business are deducted. It provides one way to evaluate how much operating profit a company generates from its revenue.

Wholesale

Selling products to another business that then sells those products to customers. EverWeave’s sales to Summit Trail Outfitters would be wholesale sales.

Direct-to-Consumer (DTC)

Selling products directly to the customer rather than through another retailer. Sales made through EverWeave’s own e-commerce operations are direct-to-consumer sales.

Working Capital

Resources available to support a company’s short-term operating needs, such as purchasing inventory, paying employees, and covering expenses while waiting to receive payment from customers or business partners.

Sales Cannibalization

When sales through a new product, location, or sales channel replace some sales that otherwise would have occurred through an existing channel rather than creating entirely new sales.

Wholesale Revenue

Revenue generated by selling products to another business for resale to its customers. Wholesale revenue may have different costs and profit margins than revenue from products sold directly to consumers.

Contingency

Money or resources reserved to help a business respond to unexpected costs, delays, or other circumstances that were not fully anticipated in the original plan.
