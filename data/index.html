Gross margin represents the percentage of sales revenue remaining after subtracting the direct cost of the products sold. It does not include all operating expenses and should not be interpreted as EverWeave’s final profit from a sales channel.

Based on the proposed pricing, volume, and product assortment, Marcus estimates that Summit sales could generate a gross margin of approximately 30–34 percent. The final margin would depend on negotiated pricing, product mix, manufacturing costs, shipping responsibilities, promotional support, and the efficiency of fulfilling Summit’s orders.

Estimated Summit gross margin: 30–34%. This range is a planning estimate; the agreement has not been finalized.

Existing independent-retailer margin: approximately 34%

Estimated Summit margin: approximately 30–34%

Summit may generate a similar or slightly lower margin percentage, but on much greater concentrated volume.

DTC produces the strongest margin, but Summit could offer national exposure and scale.

A lower margin percentage does not automatically make Summit unattractive, just as higher revenue does not automatically make it profitable.

EverWeave already has experience selling products through independent retailers. The Summit proposal, however, would introduce a 300-store national network, substantially increase the scale of the company’s wholesale business, and concentrate significant purchasing volume in one retail partner—an undertaking much larger than EverWeave’s existing wholesale relationships.

Wholesale sales generally produce less revenue per unit than direct-to-consumer sales, although they may provide substantially greater volume and broader market exposure.

Putting the Numbers in Perspective

Summit’s proposal represents approximately $1.8 million in annual wholesale purchases. That amount would be revenue to EverWeave—not profit.

The $1.8 million in annual wholesale revenue cannot be compared directly with the $700,000 preparation and early implementation investment to determine profit. EverWeave would also incur the ongoing costs of producing, fulfilling, and supporting the Summit business.

Revenue is the money generated from selling products before the costs of producing those products and operating the business are deducted.

EverWeave uses its revenue to pay for products and manufacturing, employee compensation, facilities, technology, marketing, insurance, shipping, and other operating expenses. The company must also maintain sufficient resources to support current operations and future growth.

EverWeave currently generates approximately $750,000 in operating income from $8.6 million in annual revenue, resulting in an operating margin of approximately 8.7 percent.

In other words, every dollar of additional revenue does not become a dollar available to the owners.

The Summit opportunity could create meaningful new revenue for EverWeave. It would also require the company to commit resources before the founders know exactly how the partnership will perform.
